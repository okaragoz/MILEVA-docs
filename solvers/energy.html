On this page

Energy equation ​

Temperature is advanced in two parts each step: transport by the flow (on markers, or on the grid with an upwind or TVD scheme), then heating and conduction on the grid. This page covers the solver for the second part, diffuse_temperature in solvers/diffuse_temperature.jl. The equation it solves, the conservative radial fluxes and the wall conditions are described in Temperature transport and time stepping.

Schemes ​

Written for the discrete conduction operator L and heat source H,

(I−θΔtL)Tn+1=(I+(1−θ)ΔtL)Tn+ΔtHρcp,

The 2-D matrix L has five entries per row (two radial, two azimuthal neighbours, periodic in θ). It is cached together with the grid, the diffusivity profile and the measure exponent, and rebuilt only if one of them changes. Its LU factor is cached as well and reused while Δt and θ are unchanged, which happens in fixed-step benchmarks but rarely under CFL control. The wall temperatures enter the right-hand side as sources with the full Δt, since they are constant over the step.

(A) Eigenvalues λ of the conduction matrix L that diffuse_temperature builds and caches (_diff_L_cache), on a 16 × 64 spherical annulus, in units of the explicit stability estimate Δtex of the next figure. All are real and negative; the stiffest reaches −λΔtex=1.9, just inside the forward-Euler limit of 2. (B) Amplification factor per step, G=[1+(1−θ)Δtλ]/(1−θΔtλ), of every eigenmode at Δt=4Δtex. Forward Euler leaves the stable band for the stiff modes; Crank-Nicolson stays in it but turns them negative; backward Euler damps them more than the exact decay. Script: docs/scripts/solvers_energy_sections.jl.

Accuracy and stability ​

Error of the three 2-D schemes after the same model time, against the time step in units of the explicit stability estimate Δtex=1/[κ(2/Δr2+2/(r1Δθ)2)]. Spherical annulus f=0.5, 32×128 cells, walls at 1300 and 300 K, a 200 K cos⁡4θ anomaly; the reference is Crank-Nicolson with Δtex/100. Forward Euler diverges just above Δtex (its point at 2Δtex is off the scale). Script: docs/scripts/solvers_energy.jl.

The figure shows the three properties that set the default:

  • Order. Crank-Nicolson is second order in time, its error falling by a factor of four when the step halves; forward and backward Euler are first order. At the same step Crank-Nicolson is two to three orders of magnitude more accurate here.

  • Stability. Both implicit schemes are unconditionally stable, so in 2-D the time step is set by advection alone (the CFL condition of compute_timestep), and there is no diffusive limit.

  • Cost. One sparse solve with a five-point matrix, which is small next to the Stokes solve of the same step.

Backward Euler is more strongly damping: at very large steps Crank-Nicolson can leave a slowly decaying, sign-alternating component in the sharpest modes, which backward Euler removes. Use θ=1 for steady-state searches with large steps and keep θ=0.5 for transient runs.

3-D steps

The explicit 3-D update is stable for Δt≲Δxmin2/(6κ). On a stretched grid with thin boundary cells keep cfg.dtMax below that bound.

Coupling the 3-D patches ​

In 3-D, conduction across the patch boundaries is handled by a short Schwarz iteration of its own. yy_compute_T_wall_bcs samples the peer temperature at each patch's θ-wall faces; both patches are re-diffused with those Dirichlet values (bc_T_jmin, bc_T_jmax keywords), cfg.nTSchwarz times per step (default 3); yy_sync_T_walls! then overwrites each patch's wall-cell row with the peer value at that cell centre. See Yin-Yang Schwarz coupling.

Largest difference between Yin's wall-row temperature and Yang's temperature at the same points, measured before the final yy_sync_T_walls!, on a 3-D shell with Nr=Nθ=12, Nφ=36. Both patches start from the same field, a conductive profile and a 300 K anomaly centred on Yin's lower θ-wall; the start difference is the bilinear sampling error. Diffused with insulated walls the patches drift apart; with the exchange of the time loop they agree to about 0.1 K after two steps. On this smooth problem one sweep does as well as the default three. Script: docs/scripts/solvers_energy_sections.jl.

Energy conservation ​

The solver is written so that the discrete energy budget closes: the radial fluxes are differences across cell faces divided by the exact cell measure, and the wall heat flow reported by the diagnostics is the flux the solver itself used. tests/test_nusselt_conduction.jl checks a one-step budget residual of order 10−15 and Nu = 1 on the solver's own conducting state; the logged budget is described in Energy budget.

(A) Change of thermal_energy over one call of diffuse_temperature minus Δt times the wall power of boundary_power, weighted with the scheme's own θ, relative to the surface power; 32 × 128 spherical annulus, Δt=5Δtex, 30 consecutive steps from a perturbed conductive field. The residual stays at 10−13, round-off for sums over 4096 cells. (B) Nusselt number from compute_diagnostics on the solver's own conducting steady state, f=0.55: top and bottom values coincide and converge to 1 at second order. Script: docs/scripts/solvers_energy_sections.jl.
