Boussinesq, TALA and ALA (2-D) ​

The same basally heated, isoviscous shell solved under the three formulations of the mass and energy equations that cfg.compressibilityMode offers, at three dissipation numbers. The page shows what each formulation changes in the flow and the temperature, and checks a property every steady compressible solution must have: the heat made by viscous dissipation equals the work done against the adiabatic term.

The three formulations ​

modemass balancedensity in the heat capacityadiabatic heating −ρrefαgTvrviscous dissipation Φ
"Boussinesq"∇⋅v=0ρ0nono
"TALA"∇⋅(ρrefv)=0ρref(r)nono
"ALA"∇⋅(ρrefv)=0ρref(r)yesyes

Under both anelastic modes the stress is the compressible deviator and the buoyancy is ρrefgαδT, with δT the lateral anomaly of T−Tad. The two O(Di) heat terms are switched on together by cfg.useShearHeating; the driver sets it for ALA only. The full equations are in Governing equations.

The names are not King et al.'s

In King et al. (2010) the truncated anelastic liquid approximation (TALA) keeps adiabatic heating and dissipation and drops only the dynamic-pressure part of the buoyancy. MILEVA's ALA also has a buoyancy that depends on temperature alone, so MILEVA's ALA is the closer match to King et al.'s TALA. MILEVA's "TALA" is an anelastic model with the O(Di) heating switched off, a formulation that the published benchmarks do not cover. Use it to separate the effect of the reference density from that of the heat terms, as on this page, not as a physical model of a mantle.

The model ​

The shell of the other examples (f=0.5495, D=1000 km), free slip, isothermal walls at Tsurf=273 K and TCMB=3273 K, so ΔT=3000 K and Tsurf/ΔT=0.091, the value of King et al. (2010). The dissipation number

Di=αgDcp,cp=1250 J kg−1K−1

is set through a constant expansivity, and the viscosity follows so that the surface Rayleigh number Ra=ρ0gαΔTD3/(κη) stays at 105. With a Grüneisen parameter of 1 and the adiabat started at the surface temperature, the reference state is King et al.'s, Tad=TsurfeDiz/D and ρref=ρ0eDiz/D: the density at the CMB is 1.28, 1.65 and 2.72 times the surface density at Di = 0.25, 0.5 and 1.

cfg.kappaDepthFactor = 0 matters here. The default ALA diffusivity grows toward the CMB as κ(1+0.5z2) (with z the normalised depth), which would make the anelastic runs differ from Boussinesq in a second way.

The checks. In every run Nutop and Nubot agree to better than 1 %, and the energy budget residual stays at or below 0.001 of the surface power. Under ALA the dissipation equals the work of the adiabatic term to 0.6, 1.0 and 2.1 % at Di = 0.25, 0.5 and 1 (panel D). In the continuous equations the two are equal in any steady state, because the anelastic mass balance makes ∫ρrefvr vanish on every shell, so the adiabatic work equals the buoyancy work, which equals the dissipation. The remaining gap is therefore discretisation error at 64 × 512. It grows with Di; a resolution study has not been run.

ALA: compressibility slows the flow. The interior follows the adiabat. Between 300 and 700 km depth its temperature rises at 0.24, 0.68 and 1.66 K/km, which is 73, 89 and 97 % of the adiabatic gradient DiT/D (0.33, 0.76 and 1.72 K/km at the mid-depth temperature). That adiabatic rise uses up part of the 3000 K across the shell, so less temperature difference is left to drive the flow, and a rising parcel cools as it expands. Nusselt number, velocity and surface heat flow all fall as Di grows: Nutop drops from 9.36 (Boussinesq) to 4.35 at Di=1. The same trend, convection weakening with Di, is what King et al. (2010) report for their anelastic cases in a Cartesian box. Their numbers do not apply to the annulus, so they are not compared here.

TALA: the reference density alone does the opposite. Without the two heat terms the interior stays isothermal, as in Boussinesq: the gradient between 300 and 700 km is −0.05 K/km at Di=1, against an adiabatic 1.88. The only effect left is the reference density. It rises with depth, so the buoyancy ρrefgαδT and the conductivity ρrefcpκ are both larger at depth than the surface values that define Ra. The deep mantle therefore convects as if Ra were higher, and Nutop, Vrms and the interior temperature all rise with Di. This is why MILEVA's TALA is not a model of a compressible mantle: dropping the heat terms removes the effect that matters most.

Nusselt numbers are measured against the conductive state of each run's own operator. Under ALA and TALA that conductive profile depends on ρref, so Nu values at different Di are not ratios to the same reference. Compare the surface heat flow qsurf as well.

None of the studies below uses this shell, so they are compared by the direction of each effect, not by its size.

So MILEVA's ALA behaves as published compressible models do: adding adiabatic heating and dissipation slows the flow and lowers the heat flow. The speed-up under MILEVA's TALA has no published reference. It is consistent with the Gassmöller et al. result above: the mass balance alone changes little, so the speed-up comes from the reference density raising the buoyancy and the conductivity at depth while no adiabatic cooling acts against it.

Running it ​

bash
export GKSwstype=nul
julia --project=. -t 2 examples/annulus_compressible_2d.jl ALA 0.5 1e5 64

Arguments are mode Di Ra Nr; mode is Boussinesq, TALA or ALA. Output goes to output/examples/compressible_ALA_Di0.5_Ra1e+05_N64/. At the end the driver prints the window means of the table and, under ALA, the adiabatic and dissipation powers with their ratio.

Each run took 5 to 8 hours of wall time on a 10-core Mac mini with four of them sharing the machine. Shorten a run with MILEVA_MAXTIME_MYR or MILEVA_MAXSTEPS. The figures come from

bash
julia --project=. docs/scripts/fig_compressible.jl output/examples
