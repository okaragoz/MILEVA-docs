Stagnant lid that fails: Arrhenius viscosity and a yield stress (2-D) ​

When viscosity falls steeply with temperature, the cold top boundary layer becomes too stiff to take part in the flow. It stops moving and thickens into a conductive lid, and convection continues underneath it in a nearly isoviscous interior, driven by the small part of the temperature drop the mobile sublayer at the base of the lid can carry. This is the one-plate regime of terrestrial planets without plate tectonics. A real lid is not infinitely strong, though: once the stresses in it reach the strength of rock it can break, sink into the mantle and be replaced, which is the episodic regime. The example sets up both ingredients, an Arrhenius viscosity and a yield stress, in the spherical annulus, at a strength where the lid holds for hundreds of Myr and then fails. The viscosity-contrast example shows how the same shell moves from a mobile to a stagnant lid as the contrast grows, without yielding.

The model ​

Boussinesq, heated uniformly from within, with the core held at a fixed temperature, free slip and isothermal walls, in the common shell. The internal heating is what lets the lid reach a steady thickness: the lid grows until it conducts away what the mantle produces. Heated from below alone, the interior sits just under the core temperature, the bottom boundary layer carries a Nusselt number of about one at this Rayleigh number and activation energy (Solomatov & Moresi 2000), and the lid thickens by conduction without levelling off. The viscosity comes from the simple path, compute_viscosity in physics/compute_viscosity.jl, which main_SphCon uses whenever cfg.useCompositeRheology is false:

η(T)=ηrefexp[ER(1T−1Tref)],ηmin≤η≤ηmax,

with every layer reference viscosity (mat.*_eta0) set to ηref, mat.E_act_scale =E/R in kelvin and mat.V_act_scale =0 (no pressure dependence). ηref is set from the interior Rayleigh number

Rai=ρ0gαΔTD3κηref.

Between the 300 K surface and the 1600 K interior the unclamped law spans about forty orders of magnitude, so the lid sits on the clamp and the effective viscosity contrast is ηmax/ηref, the third argument.

On top of that, cfg.useSimpleYield caps the stress at a yield stress τy (the fifth argument), the pseudo-plastic lid of Moresi & Solomatov (1998):

ηeff=min(η(T), τy2ε˙II),

with ε˙II the second invariant of the strain rate. The cap is applied by simple_yield! in physics/compute_viscosity.jl, which reads the cohesion, friction and mat.maxYieldStress; the driver sets the cohesion and the cap to τy and the friction to zero, so the yield stress is the same at every depth. Where the lid stress stays below τy nothing changes. Where it would exceed it, the viscosity drops until the stress equals τy, and since that viscosity depends on the strain rate it produces, each step iterates Stokes solve, strain rate and viscosity (Picard) until the velocity settles.

Which Arrhenius law

The time loop applies the Arrhenius law in compute_viscosity, driven by mat.E_act_scale and mat.T_ref, which is what this example sets. The separate cfg.useArrhenius helper (utils/arrhenius_viscosity.jl) is not used by main_SphCon.

parametervalue
E300 kJ/mol (mat.E_act_scale = 36 080 K)
Tref1600 K
Rai106 (first argument); ηref=1.36×1021 Pa s
clampsηmin=ηref/10, ηmax=ηref⋅Δη=1.36×1025 Pa s with Δη=104 (third argument)
yield stressτy=8 MPa, depth independent (fifth argument; cfg.useSimpleYield)
internal heatingH=10−11 W/kg, uniform (fourth argument; cfg.useInternalHeating, cfg.internalHeating_H), about 20 mW/m² of heat production per unit surface
Tcmb, Tsurf1700 K (held fixed), 300 K
initial stateinterior at 1600 K, exponential boundary layers of 100 km (top) and 50 km (CMB), a degree-4 CMB perturbation of 20 K and 5 K noise
gridNr × 8 Nr (default 64 × 512)
run length1500 Myr (cfg.maxTimeMyr)
solverMUMPS direct, markers for temperature

The driver as documented (Rai=106, Δη=104, H=10−11 W/kg, τy=8 MPa, 64 × 512), 8854 steps from 0 to 1500 Myr, 114 frames at 5 frames per second. VTK output is written every 8 steps, about 5 Myr apart while the lid is quiet; the animation keeps one frame per 20 Myr there and one in every 16 outputs while the lid fails, when the steps are much shorter, so each failure plays in slow motion. Left: temperature, shown between 1100 and 1800 K; everything colder than 1100 K, the lid, is the saturated dark blue band. Right: log10 viscosity, from about 1020 Pa s in the hottest interior to the ηmax clamp of 1.4×1025 Pa s in the lid. The lid thickens for 200 Myr, breaks at 214 Myr and sinks into the mantle within about 2 Myr, and a new lid grows over the cooled interior. It fails again, more slowly, between 841 and 856 Myr, with shorter bursts of surface motion at about 868 and 987 Myr, and is still regrowing at 1500 Myr.

The same run at 1, 214, 846 and 1497 Myr, same colour range: the lid before it fails, the first failure (a cold slab of lid sinking at the left), the second, and the regrown lid over an interior still cooler than before the first failure.

Running it ​

bash
export GKSwstype=nul
julia --project=. -t 4 examples/annulus_stagnant_lid_2d.jl 1e6 64 1e4 1e-11 8

Arguments are Ra_i Nr Δη H τ_y, with H in W/kg and τy in MPa; H = 0 heats the shell from below only and τ_y = 0 switches yielding off. These are also the defaults, so the bare command runs the documented case. At 64 × 512 the run above took about 14 h of wall time on two threads (5.7 s per step, Picard sweeps included) with another long run sharing the machine; the smoke test below gives an uncontended timing. At the end the driver prints the means of lid_mobility, lid_thick_km and Nu_top over the last third of the rows of diagnostics.csv.

What to expect ​

Whether the lid survives depends on how its stresses compare with τy. The same case was run at four yield stresses (all at Δη=104, H=10−11 W/kg, 64 × 512, to 1500 Myr or beyond):

τylid failuresbehaviour
10 MPanonestagnant; the lid levels off at 252 km after about 1300 Myr, lid_mobility 3×10−4
8 MPa (documented)214 Myr, 841 to 856 Myr, shorter bursts at 868 and 987 Myrepisodic; the lid regrows to 290 km by 1500 Myr
7 MPa164 Myr, 1017 Myr, and a smaller burst at 309 Myrepisodic
5 MPa131 Myr, repeatedly between 283 and 454 Myr, 1150 Myrepisodic, failures more frequent

In the documented case each failure shows as a burst in every column of diagnostics.csv: Vrms_cmyr rises from about 0.01 to 100 cm/yr at the first failure, the surface moves with the mantle (lid_mobility near 1), lid_thick_km collapses from 191 to 15 km, and q_surf_mWm2 peaks at about 410 mW/m². The sinking lid then fills the mantle with cold rock: the mean temperature falls from 1380 K at the first failure to 1160 K after the second, below the 1700 K core, so heat now flows out of the core. While the new lid is still thin, between about 350 and 420 Myr, the surface keeps moving at a few cm/yr before the lid stiffens again. In the quiet stretches the whole mantle is nearly still, which makes lid_mobility, a ratio of two tiny velocities, read 0.5 or so; read Vsurf_rms_cmyr (0.02 cm/yr or less) to see that the surface is not moving. Over 1000 to 1500 Myr the documented case gives:

quantityvalue
lid_thick_km (1300 K isotherm)243 km, growing
Vsurf_rms_cmyr0.0075 cm/yr
Vrms_cmyr0.013 cm/yr
q_surf_mWm225.4 mW/m²
q_cmb_mWm217.4 mW/m² (out of the core)

The energy budget closes to better than one part in a thousand per sample between failures (budget_resid); during a failure the steps are so short that the sampled interval misses most of the change and the residual rises to about 0.2 for a few samples. On 178 steps, during the failures and the recoveries after them, the Picard iteration stopped at its 50-sweep cap with the residual above its tolerance of 10−4 (37 such steps by step 2400, where the log reports 1.2×10−4, and 178 by step 7200, late in the second failure, at 1.6×10−3); picard_n and picard_rel in diagnostics.csv record it. With no yield stress (τ_y = 0) the lid can only thin by convective erosion, and the run sits in the stagnant column of the table at any contrast of 104 or more.

Smoke test ​

A quick check that the driver runs:

bash
MILEVA_MAXSTEPS=6 julia --project=. -t 2 examples/annulus_stagnant_lid_2d.jl 1e6 16

6 steps at 16 × 128 with the default contrast, heating and yield stress in 37 s of wall time (mostly compilation), 2.2 GB; the driver reports a lid of 153 km and a lid mobility of 10−3. At 64 × 512, 40 steps took 0.70 s per step and 2.7 GB on two threads. Steps while the lid fails cost more, because the Picard iteration then needs up to tens of sweeps instead of two.
