Primordial and recycled material in the lower mantle (2-D) ​

The lower mantle may hold two kinds of chemically distinct material: primordial rock left over from the planet's early differentiation, and basaltic crust recycled from the surface. Gülcher, Ballmer & Tackley (2021) asked how the two coexist over the age of the Earth and what decides whether the primordial material survives. They started from a dense, viscous, bridgmanite-enriched layer that fills the lower half of the mantle, and followed it for 4.5 Gyr while convection, melting and crustal recycling went on above it. This example repeats their reference case M100dD in the spherical annulus.

Temperature (300 to 4000 K) and the fraction of primordial material (0 to 1) over 4.5 Gyr, Nr = 64 (64 × 512 cells).

Set-up ​

shellr = 3481 to 6371 km, free slip, net rotation removed
walls300 K at the surface, 4000 K at the core-mantle boundary, no internal heating
reference stateanelastic liquid, Perple_X pyrolite table, adiabat from 1900 K
viscosityArrhenius diffusion creep with a pressure-dependent yield stress (below)
primordial layerthe lowest 1650 km, 5 % of it ambient mantle, density excess 63.3 kg m⁻³, 100 times more viscous
ambient mantle15 % basalt, melting and crustal recycling on
initial stateadiabat with thermal boundary layers, a degree-6 perturbation of 20 K and 10 K noise
run4.5 Gyr

The viscosity is Newtonian and depends on temperature and pressure,

η=η0λCexp⁡[Ea+pVaRT−EaRT0],ηeff=min(η, τy2ε˙II),τy=30 MPa+0.01p,

with η0=5×1020 Pa s at T0 = 1600 K, Ea = 140 kJ mol⁻¹, Va = 1.8 cm³ mol⁻¹, and λC = 100 for primordial material and 1 otherwise. The yield stress lets a cold lid break where it is stressed enough.

The density excess of the primordial material is set by the buoyancy ratio

B=ΔρCραΔTsa=0.28,

evaluated at 1500 km depth on the reference adiabat (ρ = 4913 kg m⁻³, α=1.77×10−5 K⁻¹), with ΔTsa = 2597 K the temperature drop across the shell minus the adiabatic increase. Primordial material that crosses the pyroxenite solidus, T>920∘C+130∘CGPa−1p, melts and becomes 40 % basalt and 60 % harzburgite, as in the paper. It then no longer counts as primordial. The fraction that survives is the preservation factor

χ(t)=Vprim(t)Vprim(0),

with the volume integrated using the shell's measure r2drdθ.

Stagnant lid, overturn and mixing ​

Temperature (top) and primordial material (bottom) at 0, 1.6, 2.25, 2.6, 3.2 and 4.5 Gyr.

A: lid mobility (rms surface velocity over rms velocity of the whole mantle) and rms velocity. B: surface heat flow. C: primordial volume, whole mantle and below 660 km, as a percentage of its initial value. Computed by docs/scripts/fig_primordial.jl from diagnostics.csv and the 245 VTK frames.

The run has three phases.

Two layers under a stagnant lid, 0 to 2.1 Gyr. The upper mantle convects under a lid that does not move (mobility below 0.01), while the dense, viscous primordial layer barely deforms. The layer is heated from the core and loses little heat through its top, so it warms, and the surface heat flow decays from 60 to 22 mW m⁻².

Overturn, 2.1 to 2.6 Gyr. Once the primordial layer is warm enough, its thermal buoyancy overcomes part of its compositional excess. Broad hot domes rise from it, reach the base of the lid and break it. The lid mobility jumps to 2 to 3.5, the rms velocity rises tenfold, and the surface heat flow peaks at 135 mW m⁻² near 2.5 Gyr. The hot primordial material that reaches shallow depth melts, so the preserved volume begins to fall.

Plate-like convection and mixing, 2.6 to 4.5 Gyr. The lid stays mobile (mobility 1.4 to 2.5), the mantle cools from 2180 to 1870 K, and the primordial material is drawn out into ridges on the core-mantle boundary, streaks and smaller blobs. By 4.5 Gyr 18 % of the primordial volume survives in the whole mantle and 13.5 % below 660 km.

The sequence is the paper's: two-layer convection under a low-mobility lid, a compositional overturn that switches the lid to plate-like behaviour, and partial preservation of the primordial material afterwards. The overturn comes about three times later than in M100dD, though within the 0.5 to 2.5 Gyr the paper reports across its cases. A later overturn leaves less time for mixing and a cooler, slower mantle at 4.5 Gyr, which is the sense of the other differences in the table.

The set-up differs from the paper's in ways that bear on the overturn time:

  • the radial grid is uniform, 45 km at Nr = 64, where the paper refines to 10 km at the boundary layers and around 660 km;

  • the viscosity factor of primordial material applies at every depth, not only in the lower mantle, and mixed cells use an arithmetic rather than a geometric mean;

  • there is no weak post-perovskite and no deflection of the phase boundaries;

  • densities come from a pyrolite table plus a constant compositional excess, and melting uses the MILEVA peridotite chain.

The driver header lists these in full.

Running it ​

bash
MILEVA_OUTDIR=output/primordial MILEVA_MAXTIME_MYR=4500 MILEVA_VTKEVERY=80 \
    julia --project=. -t 2 examples/annulus_primordial_recycled_2d.jl 64
julia --project=. docs/scripts/fig_primordial.jl output/primordial

Further arguments set B, λC and the initial layer thickness in km. The run took 17 hours on 2 threads (19 557 steps).

References ​

Gülcher, A. J. P., Ballmer, M. D. & Tackley, P. J. (2021), Coupled dynamics and evolution of primordial and recycled heterogeneity in Earth's lower mantle, Solid Earth 12, 2087-2107, doi:10.5194/se-12-2087-2021.
