Thermal evolution of Mars and Mercury (2-D) ​

Mars and Mercury are one-plate planets: a thick, cold lid sits over a convecting mantle and the planet loses its heat by conduction through it. The two differ mainly in their mantles. Mars has a mantle 1550 km deep over a core about half the planet's radius; Mercury's mantle is only 420 km deep over a core that fills most of the planet. This example follows both over 4.5 Gyr in the spherical annulus, from a hot start, with decaying radiogenic heating and a core that cools or warms through the heat crossing the core-mantle boundary. Each planet is run with and without a 50 km crust that holds twice the bulk heat production.

Runs in progress

The four runs are still computing. The figures and numbers below are from the state reached so far and are updated as the runs advance.

Mars without crust at 60, 300, 690 and 1290 Myr, temperature between 1700 and 2300 K (the lid, colder than 1700 K, is the dark rim). Nr = 124 (124 × 1650 cells, 12.5 km radially). The mantle warms under its lid: the radiogenic heat is produced faster than the lid can conduct it away.

Mercury without crust at 30, 150, 300 and 540 Myr, temperature between 1500 and 2100 K. Nr = 84 (84 × 1533 cells, 5 km radially). The mantle is so thin that it holds a single row of small cells; they merge into fewer, wider ones as the lid thickens.

Set-up ​

The physical set-up follows Fleury et al. (2024, their Table 1).

MarsMercury
planet / core radius3400 / 1850 km (D=1550 km)2440 / 2020 km (D=420 km)
surface temperature220 K440 K
initial core temperature2000 K2000 K
gravity3.7 m/s²3.7 m/s²
thermal diffusivity10−6 m²/s1.04×10−6 m²/s
density core / mantle6000 / 3500 kg/m³6980 / 3380 kg/m³
heat capacity mantle / core1142 / 850 J/kg/K1142 / 850 J/kg/K
conductivity mantle / crust4 / 3 W/m/K4 / 3 W/m/K
present-day heat-producing elementsU 16 ppb, Th 58 ppb, K 309 ppmU 7 ppb, Th 29 ppb, K 550 ppm
grid124 × 165084 × 1533

Both planets share the rest:

energyextended Boussinesq: incompressible, constant density, with adiabatic heating and viscous dissipation
viscositydiffusion creep, η=ηrefexp[E+pVRT−E+prefVRTref], ηref=1021 Pa s at 1600 K and 3 GPa, E=300 kJ/mol, V=6 cm³/mol, bounded to 1018 to 1026 Pa s
boundariesfree slip, fixed surface temperature
coreρcccVcdTCMB/dt=−qcAc, no inner core
heatingU-238, U-235, Th-232 and K-40, each decaying with its own half-life from 4.5 Gyr before present
crust (crust runs)a 50 km shell with twice the bulk heat production per kilogram and conductivity 3 W/m/K; the mantle below is depleted by mass balance
initial statean adiabat from a potential temperature of 1745 K with 50 km boundary layers meeting the surface and core temperatures, plus a 20 K seed and 10 K of noise near the core

How MILEVA represents it: the extended Boussinesq equations are MILEVA's anelastic liquid approximation with a constant reference state. MILEVA's mantle heat capacity is fixed at 1250 J/kg/K, so the run scales density, gravity and heat production so that every product entering the equations (ρ0cp, ρ0g, g/cp, the heat produced per unit volume and the conductivity) is the one in the table. The crust is a fixed shell defined by radius: it changes heat production and conductivity and nothing else. There is no melting, no compositional buoyancy and no free surface.

Running it ​

bash
julia --project=. -t 2 examples/annulus_fleury_thermal_evolution_2d.jl mars 0 124 1650
julia --project=. -t 2 examples/annulus_fleury_thermal_evolution_2d.jl mercury 0 84 1533

Arguments are planet crust Nr Nt, with crust 0 or 1. Nr = 124 for Mars and 84 for Mercury put a cell face exactly at the base of the 50 km crust. Wall time on two threads with other runs sharing the machine: Mars reached 1290 Myr in 51 h, Mercury 540 Myr in 5 h. A run interrupted by a reboot continues from its last checkpoint with MILEVA_RESUME=latest.

What to expect ​

Mars. The mantle starts at a volume-mean 1760 K and a stagnant lid grows to about 140 km within the first 400 Myr; surface velocities stay below 10−4 cm/yr, so lid_mobility reads 10−5 throughout. Radiogenic heating is strongest early, and the mantle warms for the first Gyr (1760 K to about 1910 K by 1300 Myr) while the surface heat flow falls from over 100 to about 34 mW/m². Radiogenic heating warms the mantle just above the core faster than the core itself, so heat flows into the core (q_cmb_mWm2 about −8 mW/m²) and the core warms, by about 90 K in the first 1300 Myr. The mantle convects in cells of order the mantle depth under the lid, at about 2 cm/yr.

Mercury. The thin mantle cools from the start: the core loses heat from the first step (q_cmb_mWm2 about +7 mW/m² at 300 Myr) and the volume-mean temperature drops from about 1610 K to 1560 K by 300 Myr, under a lid of about 100 km, a quarter of the mantle depth. With so thin a mantle the convecting layer below the lid is only about 300 km deep, and the flow slows as the mantle cools.

The crust. The crust runs put a larger share of the heat production into the top 50 km, where it is conducted out directly instead of warming the convecting mantle. Their comparison with the runs without crust will be added when both reach 4.5 Gyr.

The energy budget in diagnostics.csv (budget_resid) stays within 1 % in all runs so far.
